# Character and World Method

## Characters are people first

A character may have a structural job, but the reader should encounter a person rather than a function wearing a name.

For each major character establish:

- surface identity and ordinary life
- private wants
- fears / avoidances
- contradictions
- competence(s)
- blind spots
- relationship-specific behavior
- what they notice that others miss
- what they misunderstand
- what changes when they enter a room
- what the story would lose if they disappeared

## Structural job stays hidden

It is useful for the author to know that a character functions as witness, destabilizer, anchor, rival, mirror, caretaker, pressure source, etc. The character should not behave like they know their structural job.

## Relationship map

Track relationships independently from individual character arcs.

For each important pair:

- what each wants from the other
- what each freely gives
- what each withholds
- what each incorrectly assumes
- what becomes easier over time
- what becomes harder
- private rituals / jokes / language
- boundary conditions
- irreversible events

## Settings are active memory containers

For each important setting record:

- sensory identity
- ordinary use
- social meaning
- economic/material reality
- what characters do there
- how it changes over time
- which memories attach to it
- continuity facts that must be tracked

A setting should be able to host an ordinary scene before it is asked to carry symbolism.
