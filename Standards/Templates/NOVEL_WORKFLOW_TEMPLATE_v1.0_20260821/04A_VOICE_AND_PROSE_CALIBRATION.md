# Voice and Prose Calibration

The outline does not own the novel's voice. The prose must be discovered and then protected.

## Why this exists

In the source project this template was derived from, the strongest proof of the method is not the workbook. It is the drafted prose: sensory detail, character-specific attention, ordinary action, and philosophical pressure emerging through lived perception rather than explanation.

A reusable workflow therefore needs an explicit **voice lock** after the first successful anchor scenes.

## Calibration samples

After drafting 2–5 anchor scenes, select 3–8 short passages that represent the book at its best.

For each passage record:

- what makes the perception character-specific
- sentence rhythm
- abstraction level
- sensory density
- dialogue density
- humor level
- distance from character consciousness
- what the prose deliberately does not explain

Do not imitate the exact sentences later. Preserve the **resolution and behavior of the voice**.

## Anti-samples

Keep 2–5 examples of prose that is technically competent but wrong for the book.

For each, explain why:

- too authorial
- too polished
- too explanatory
- too lyrical
- too sparse
- too knowing
- too genre-coded
- not character-specific

## Recalibration rule

If later prose becomes smoother but less alive, compare it to the calibration samples before revising the samples themselves.

The purpose of this document is to prevent revision from sanding away the thing that made the novel worth writing.
