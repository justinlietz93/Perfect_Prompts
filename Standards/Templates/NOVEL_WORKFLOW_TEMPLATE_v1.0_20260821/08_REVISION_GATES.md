# Revision Gates

Run these as tests, not vibes.

## Gate A — Local life

**Question:** Does every scene work as human life even if the reader knows nothing about the larger architecture?

PASS: characters want things, act, perceive, and change.

FAIL: scene exists mainly to deliver structure/theme.

## Gate B — Voice

**Question:** Could this scene belong to this protagonist and no generic protagonist?

PASS: perception, syntax, attention, and omission are character-specific.

FAIL: polished but interchangeable prose.

## Gate C — Character autonomy

**Question:** Do secondary characters have motives that exceed their service to the protagonist?

PASS: they can surprise the plot without violating themselves.

FAIL: they appear only when the structure needs a function.

## Gate D — Reader experience

**Question:** Is the reader being trained to read the book in the intended mode?

PASS: curiosity, trust, tension, uncertainty, humor, dread, romance, etc. arise at the intended scale.

FAIL: the reader is solving when they should be living, confused when they should be attached, or reassured when they should be uncertain.

## Gate E — Exposition

**Question:** Is the prose saying something the structure or behavior already shows?

PASS: explanation is necessary and character-natural.

FAIL: the author repeats the meaning after the scene delivered it.

## Gate F — Continuity

**Question:** Are all contradictions intentional and all required continuities preserved?

PASS: continuity matrix explains every known conflict.

FAIL: accidental contradictions are indistinguishable from designed ones.

## Gate G — Pressure economy

**Question:** Does each chapter materially change pressure, relationship, knowledge, or responsibility?

PASS: chapter leaves a different state than it entered.

FAIL: atmosphere repeats without transformation.

## Gate H — Reference independence

**Question:** Would the novel still possess its identity if every reference work disappeared?

PASS: reference mechanisms have been metabolized.

FAIL: scenes are recognizable borrowings with names changed.

## Gate I — Ending law

**Question:** Does the ending satisfy the Frame Lock rather than merely close the plot?

PASS: the ending is emotionally and structurally earned.

FAIL: it solves discomfort by violating the book's governing law.

## Gate J — Anti-flattening

**Question:** Has revision made the book clearer by reducing it to one sentence?

PASS: clarity improves while complexity remains lived.

FAIL: every ambiguity, contradiction, or unresolved tension has been explained away.
